# SOP-03 · Summer Survival

**Who:** Summer season owner + the volunteer rota · **When:** Set up *before* school lets out, then weekly across the break · **Time:** Setup ~half day; weekly visit ~30–45 min

> Summer is the hard one: the hottest, highest-stress months, and the school is nearly empty. The greenhouse is *designed* to survive untended — but only if it's set up right and watched from a distance. This SOP is how we keep it alive (or rest it safely) until school returns.

---

## A. Before school lets out — the setup (do this once, by late spring)

- [ ] **Decide the mode** (per [Operating Principle 3](../00-operating-principles.md)): are we **growing through summer** with a committed rota, or **resting the beds**? Don't drift — decide and write it down. *(Decision deadline: ~April.)*
- [ ] **If growing:** plant only **heat-tolerant, low-attention** crops that match the coverage we actually have.
- [ ] **If resting:** clear out tender plants, mulch/cover beds, shut down what isn't needed — but **leave the safety systems (vent/alarm) running.**
- [ ] **Deploy full shade** (shade cloth at the summer setting) — the cheapest cooling is heat you keep out.
- [ ] **Verify irrigation autonomy:** it must keep plants alive with no one present. Test a full cycle.
- [ ] **Confirm alerts go to phones that will be watched all summer** (high-temp, low-moisture, power/battery). Test each one.
- [ ] **Lock the rota:** named volunteers, dates, and a coordinator — *in writing, before school ends.* No gaps.
- [ ] **Post the escalation chain** with current summer phone numbers.

## B. The weekly summer visit

Run [SOP-02 · Weekly Check](SOP-02-weekly-check.md) in full, plus:

- [ ] **Go early or late** — never midday heat. 📷
- [ ] Vent the space *before* entering.
- [ ] **Tank level vs. the week's forecast** — top up proactively, don't wait for empty.
- [ ] **Battery trend** — summer runs the fans hard; make sure it's keeping up.
- [ ] Heat-tolerant crops still tolerating it? Pull anything failing so it doesn't rot/attract pests.

## C. Weather-watch trigger — the hot-and-cloudy stretch ⚠️

This is the danger window: **peak heat + weak solar + draining battery, all at once.** When the forecast shows a **multi-day hot *and* cloudy** stretch:

- [ ] **Pre-charge** the battery while you still have sun.
- [ ] **Shed Tier 3 loads early** (grow lights, data logging, non-essential pumps) to protect the critical loop.
- [ ] **Confirm the passive layer is ready:** sides can roll up, wax-piston vents move freely, shade is deployed. The greenhouse must survive on these alone if the battery dies.
- [ ] **Top up water** — passive cooling leans harder on ventilation, and any evaporative cooling burns water.
- [ ] **Brief the on-call volunteer** that it's a watch period; eyes on the phone alerts.
- [ ] **Consider the campus tether** (emergency only): for a forecast extreme stretch, an Amber/Red volunteer may run the temporary extension cord + garden hose from campus to back up power and water. Not a substitute for the passive layer — a bonus margin.

## D. Emergency response

> **High-temp alarm, or you arrive to a too-hot greenhouse:**
1. **Open the door and roll up the sides immediately** — vent before doing anything else. Don't linger inside a hot closed space.
2. **Are the fans/vents running?** If not → it's likely **power**: check the battery state-of-charge and the controller.
3. **Battery dead / no power →** rely on the **passive** layer: get every vent and side open, confirm shade is on. The building is built to survive on passive cooling — your job is to make sure nothing is blocking it.
4. **Still climbing →** call the maintenance contact / staff lead. For a severe spike, hand-water to protect plants and keep it open.
5. **Emergency tether →** if it's an extreme-weather event, an Amber/Red volunteer can deploy the campus power/water tether for extra margin (emergency only).

> **Volunteer heat safety:** never work alone deep in a hot greenhouse, go in the cool hours, hydrate, and step out if you feel unwell. People before plants — always.

## ✅ What "good" looks like
A greenhouse that needs you to *confirm* it's fine, not *rescue* it. Automation carrying the load, passive systems ready as backup, water and battery ahead of demand, a rota with no gaps.

## 📓 Log it
Every visit: date · name · battery & tank readings · what you did · anything off. The log is how the next volunteer in the rota picks up cleanly.
