# Site & Orientation

*The last big open question from the [design principles](00-design-principles.md). Until the site is fixed, several downstream decisions can't be — sun path, summer breeze, drainage, and where the solar array lives all flow from it. This document is a **decision framework**, not a final pick: it lays out the criteria for evaluating candidate locations on the Kramer Elementary campus and the orientation logic once one is chosen.*

## Why this gates everything

Siting is the one decision that's **physically impossible to fix later** — you can re-plumb a bed, but you can't move the building cheaply. It also feeds the [slab pre-pour checklist](../build/00-slab-prepour-checklist.md): floor plan, drainage slope, conduit runs, and the solar feed all depend on where and how the building sits.

## Dallas sun & wind facts to design against

- **Latitude ≈ 32.8 °N.** Summer noon sun is very high (~80° altitude at the solstice); winter noon sun is low (~34°). So summer heat comes from nearly overhead, winter light comes in low from the south.
- **Prevailing summer wind is from the south / south-southeast.** This is a gift for our cooling-first design — orient to *catch* that breeze for cross-ventilation.
- **Expansive clay soils (Houston Black and kin) are common in Dallas.** They shrink and swell with moisture and are a real foundation hazard — this matters as much for siting as sun does. Flagged here, handled in the slab doc.

## Orientation logic

Two defensible options; the choice follows from our priorities (cooling and resilience over peak winter yield):

| Option | Long axis | Pros | Cons |
|--------|-----------|------|------|
| **E–W long axis** | runs east–west, broad face south | Broad south face captures low **winter** light; classic for year-round growing. | Long south wall takes maximum summer solar load — more to shade. |
| **N–S long axis** | runs north–south | More even light across the day; less structural self-shading; at our latitude the summer penalty of E–W is reduced. | Slightly less winter solar capture. |

**Working recommendation:** lean **E–W long axis** so the broad sidewalls open toward the southerly summer breeze for cross-ventilation (REQ-COOL-8), *paired with aggressive shading* (REQ-LIGHT-1) to tame the summer solar load on the south face. Confirm against the final crop calendar — a summer-resting program weights this differently than a year-round one. *(Decide with the crop list; see open questions.)*

- **Ridge vents run the length of the long axis** to exhaust the hottest air via stack effect (REQ-COOL-3/4).
- **Intake (roll-up sides) face the prevailing breeze**, exhaust opposite/high.
- **Solar array faces true south, unshaded**, tilt ≈ latitude (~30–33°) for year-round, or shallower (~20°) to favor the summer cooling season when demand peaks. Ground-mount vs. roof-mount is a site call (roof adds structural/shading complexity).

## Site selection criteria

Score each candidate location on campus against these. The first three are near-disqualifying if they fail.

### Tier 1 — make-or-break
1. **Solar exposure.** Unshaded southern sky for both the greenhouse glazing *and* the PV array. Watch for shade from the school building, mature trees, and rooflines — and remember winter shadows are long. *Off-grid lives or dies here.*
2. **Drainage & soil.** Naturally drains away from the pad; not a low spot that ponds. Assess the expansive-clay risk early — it drives slab cost (REQ-SLAB-2/6).
3. **Safe, supervised access for children.** Short, safe walking path from classrooms; visible from staff areas for passive supervision; away from drives, loading zones, and traffic.

### Tier 2 — strongly weighted
4. **Prevailing-breeze access** — sidewalls can open to the southerly summer wind, not blocked by a wall or building.
5. **Water source nearby** — for rainwater-tank siting and any backup supply (REQ-WATER-5).
6. **ADA-friendly grade** — a route that can meet accessible slope without heroic earthwork (REQ-ACC-1).
7. **Delivery/construction access** — can a concrete truck and materials reach it?
8. **Security** — visible enough to deter vandalism; consider lighting and fencing.

### Tier 3 — nice to have
9. Proximity to a hose bib, existing utilities (even though we're off-grid, water/temporary power help construction).
10. Room to expand (phaseability, per Design Principle 6).
11. Visibility as a **showcase** from the street or entry (Mission 5) — last, per the priority hierarchy.
